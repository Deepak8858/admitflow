# AdmitFlow pilot launch kit

Prepared 2 October 2026. Status: launch candidate, not a production release or announcement record. No messages have been sent.

## Launch decision

Start with a scoped coaching-institute pilot enquiry campaign. The goal is one paying institute after demonstrating fit, not maximizing unqualified registrations. The candidate homepage makes “Discuss a pilot” primary while retaining account creation. Pricing and contact copy explain scope before rollout without promising a free trial, fixed implementation timeline or revenue lift.

Target: owner-led Indian coaching institutes with a small counselling team, existing enquiries and a clear follow-up problem. Do not present this target definition as a measured market statistic.

Offer: an initial workflow discussion and fictional-data walkthrough; agree the team, enquiry cohort, duration, success measures, connected services, commercial terms and support responsibilities before live use. No student records or provider credentials in first-contact email.

Qualification questions: How do enquiries arrive? Who owns follow-up? What gets missed? Which CRM or WhatsApp setup is in use? Who can authorize a pilot? What outcome would justify paying to continue?

## Revenue experiment

Use paid pilot discussions to test willingness to pay, but do not publish a price before checking provider costs and support capacity. Track three explicit outcomes: not a fit, interested but not ready, and commercial discussion requested. A reply or account creation is not revenue. Revenue reporting requires actual received payments and separate refunds.

Before quoting: verify the legal seller, invoice/payment route, included seats and usage, messaging/AI charges, support workload and cancellation terms. Propose a scoped amount only after these inputs exist. No invented scarcity, reserved places or discount deadline.

## Demo: five minutes

1. Describe a fictional enquiry with no named owner.
2. Show enquiry entry/CSV preview and assignment.
3. Open shared conversation context and explain human takeover.
4. Show counselling booking; describe one-way Google Calendar updates accurately.
5. Show recorded admission receipt and separate refund reporting.
6. Ask which handoff matches the institute's current problem.

Use fictional data. A demonstration is not proof of live WhatsApp, billing or tenant acceptance.

### 2. LinkedIn: practical checklist

Before buying another admissions tool, pick ten recent enquiries and ask:

Who owns each one? What is the next action? When is it due? Has the student agreed to the contact channel? Where will the outcome be recorded?

If the answers are spread across personal chats and a spreadsheet, that is a workflow worth examining.

We published a checklist for coaching teams:
https://admitflow.incfrog.ai/resources/coaching-admissions-follow-up-checklist?utm_source=linkedin&utm_medium=social

Disclosure: I build AdmitFlow, the product behind this guide.

### 3. LinkedIn: measurement

A replied message, a counselling booking and an admission are three different outcomes.

For an admissions pilot, agree the enquiry group and baseline first. Track delivery, replies, bookings, attendance and recorded admissions separately. Keep refunds separate too.

A before/after increase is useful evidence, but it does not by itself prove the software caused the increase.

Here is the approach we propose for AdmitFlow pilots:
